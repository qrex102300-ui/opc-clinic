Free founder diagnostic

Users show up. Nobody pays. Which problem do you actually have?

“Zero paying customers” is a symptom, not a diagnosis. Before you rebuild onboarding, cut your price, add features, or start another marketing channel, locate the first broken step in the chain.

Rule: diagnose the earliest broken step. A founder with zero qualified traffic and a founder with 100 daily active users can both have $0 revenue, but they do not have the same problem.

The 5-minute decision tree

1 · Reach

Are enough plausible buyers seeing the product?

If almost nobody in the intended buyer group reaches the site, demo, or offer, the first hypothesis is distribution. Do not call this a pricing problem yet.

7-day test: pick one narrow buyer group, contact or reach a defined number of them through one channel, and measure qualified conversations or visits — not likes or impressions.

2 · Activation

Do signups reach the first meaningful outcome?

If people sign up but fail before experiencing the core value, the likely bottleneck is activation / onboarding. More traffic only pours more people into a leak.

7-day test: define one activation event, measure the percentage of new users who reach it, then remove one major step or personally onboard a small cohort and compare.

3 · Value

Do activated users come back or use the product again?

If users reach the promised outcome once but rarely return, the problem may be frequency or enduring value. A one-time-use product can be useful and still be a poor subscription.

7-day test: measure repeat usage among activated users and interview the ones who do not return. Test a pricing model that matches actual usage frequency before adding subscription-only features.

4 · Monetization

Do repeat users understand why the paid version is worth money?

If the right users repeatedly receive value but almost nobody upgrades, test packaging, pricing, paywall timing, or paid-value clarity.

7-day test: show a concrete paid offer to active users and track offer views → checkout starts → purchases. Change one variable at a time so you can falsify the hypothesis.

5 · Buyer / Sales

Do demos get praise but no company buys?

Positive feedback without purchase can mean wrong buyer, weak urgency, no budget authority, or an ineffective sales motion. “They liked the demo” is not willingness to pay.

7-day test: narrow to one buyer role and one urgent job. Ask for a real next commitment — paid pilot, procurement step, or specific decision date — instead of another feedback call.

Why this matters now

Recent founder reports show the same $0-revenue symptom with different causes. On Sep 8, 2026, a B2B ATS founder described a working product, outreach, HR conversations, demos, and positive feedback but zero paying customers — leaving buyer, segment, and sales motion unresolved. Another SaaS with 100+ daily uses reported almost no paid conversion because the core use case was often one-time, making subscription frequency itself questionable. Separately, an AI-startup experiment with seven shipped products reported zero revenue across all seven after the build phase, explicitly naming distribution as the wall.
