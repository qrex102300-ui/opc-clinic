B2B post-demo diagnostic

Prospects like the demo. Nobody buys. What is actually broken?

Positive feedback is not purchase intent. If demos end with compliments but not money, do not automatically add features or lower price. First identify which commitment step is failing.

Rule: replace “Did they like it?” with “What real commitment did they make next?” A paid pilot, procurement step, decision date, internal introduction, or budget discussion is evidence. Praise alone is not.

Five bottlenecks that look identical from the outside

1 · Buyer

Wrong person: the demo attendee feels the pain but cannot approve spend. Test: run the next conversations with one role that owns both the problem and a budget path. Ask who signs or blocks the purchase before the demo ends.

2 · Urgency

Real problem, weak timing: the product is useful but not urgent enough to displace current workarounds. Test: ask for the cost of doing nothing for 30 days and offer a paid pilot tied to that cost. If there is no credible cost or deadline, urgency is the leading suspect.

3 · Proof

They believe the idea, not the outcome: the buyer still lacks proof that it works in their environment. Test: replace another feature demo with one narrow outcome proof, sample result, or measurable pilot success criterion.

4 · Offer / Price

Value is clear but the buying package is not: scope, risk, contract size, or pricing model may be misaligned. Test: present one concrete paid pilot with fixed scope, fixed price, and a defined success threshold; measure acceptance, objection type, and checkout/procurement progress.

5 · Sales motion

The meeting never asks for a decision: repeated “feedback calls” can create applause without a buying event. Test: every qualified demo ends with one explicit next commitment and date. Track how many prospects accept a concrete next step instead of “keep me posted.”

Why this is a real founder problem

On September 8, 2026, a founder publicly described a working AI ATS, LinkedIn outreach, HR conversations, demos and positive feedback — yet zero paying customers, with uncertainty about segment, buyer and sales motion. The important point is not that this founder needs the same answer as everyone else. It is that the visible symptom does not reveal the cause.
